## 📊 Project Overview

This project presents an interactive **Diabetes Analysis Dashboard** created using **Tableau Public**. The dashboard explores patterns and relationships between age group, BMI category, glucose, insulin, and diabetes status.

## 📈 Visualizations

The dashboard contains four visualizations:

- 🫧 Bubble Chart – Compares average glucose and BMI based on diabetes status.
- 📊 Bar Chart – Shows outcomes across different age groups.
- 🔥 Heat Map – Shows the distribution of BMI categories across age groups.
- 📦 Box Plot – Compares insulin levels between diabetic and non-diabetic groups.

## 🔍 Key Findings

### 1. Age Group 25–34 Has the Highest Outcome
The 25–34 age group has the highest outcome value, at approximately **95**, compared with the other age groups.

### 2. Obesity Is Highest in the 25–34 Age Group
The heat map shows that the **25–34 age group has the highest obese count (21,534)**. The 35–44 age group also has a high obese count of **14,312**.

### 3. Diabetic Group Shows Greater Insulin Variation
The box plot indicates that the **Diabetic group has a wider range of insulin values** and more extreme values compared with the Non-Diabetic group.

## 💡 Suggestions and Recommendations

1. **Focus on the 25–34 Age Group**
   - Promote health awareness, regular physical activity, balanced diets, and diabetes screening.

2. **Promote Early Health Screening**
   - Regular monitoring of glucose, BMI, insulin, and diabetes status can help identify people who may be at higher risk.

3. **Target Obesity Prevention**
   - Encourage healthy eating habits and regular physical activity, especially among age groups with higher obesity levels.

## 🎯 Overall Conclusion

The analysis shows that **age, BMI category, glucose, and insulin** are important factors associated with diabetes. The **25–34 age group** shows particularly high outcome and obesity values. Early health screening, healthy lifestyle awareness, and obesity prevention can help reduce diabetes risk.
